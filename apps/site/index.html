01 · the inspiration

One page was enough.

In 1976, Steve Jobs described the entire Apple-1 on a single handwritten page — the chips, the memory, the terminal, the price. Anyone could read it. Nothing was hidden. The spec was the product.

Fifty years later, your work is scattered across a hundred tabs, ten tools, and one inbox — and now your agents work in places you can’t see at all. dohc is our answer: put the whole workspace back on the page.

02 · on board

The primitives, not the apps.

dohc doesn’t ship a CRM, a tracker, or a dashboard product. It ships the sturdy pieces — and your agent assembles the workspace you actually need out of them.

a documents
Plain-text first. Everything you and your agents make is a page you can open, diff, and edit.
b entities & links
People, projects, deals, anything — typed records that link into your documents and back.
c dashboards & views
Source-backed views over your real text. No second copy of the truth to keep in sync.
d history & provenance
Every edit is recorded — who, when, and why, whether it was you or an agent.
e review gates
Agents propose; you approve. Locks and gates keep the pen in your hand.
f skills
When an agent handles something well once, keep it. Repeated work becomes a playbook.
g search
One index across documents, entities, history, and chats — because it’s all text.
h local-first
Workspaces are folders on your disk. No backend required. Leave anytime with everything.
03 · convictions

What we refuse to hide.

Text is the primitive.

A folder of well-structured text files and a capable agent could recreate most of dohc. dohc exists to make that easy, reliable, and powerful — the magic is a shared substrate humans and agents can both trust, not hidden state.

Agents wrap tools, not the other way around.

Deterministic software provides the sturdy pieces — storage, sync, permissions, history. Your agent uses them to build the workspace around your intent. Not old software with AI sprinkled in.

Individual first.

If dohc only works after a whole company adopts it, we built the wrong thing. It should feel valuable when the entire “organization” is one human and their agents.

Open when shared, private when personal.

Visibility comes from explicit boundaries, sharing, and consent — never accidental leakage. Your personal workspace is yours.
